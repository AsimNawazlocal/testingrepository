3. SELECTION METHOD AND RELEVANCE ENFORCEMENT
================================================================================
Images were tagged into ~25 topic categories from filename content (manually spot-checked
against the actual photos via contact sheets), then matched to each page's existing
IMAGE_SLOT_META placeholders and to newly found H2+paragraph insertion points using a
two-part acceptance gate:
  (a) the image must match the page's PRIMARY (most specific) topical category, OR
  (b) at least 2 distinct keywords must overlap between the image's filename and the
      surrounding section's actual text.
A match on only a broad fallback category (e.g. "general_pipe_repair") plus one generic
shared word was explicitly NOT accepted — this rule was added after an initial draft
allocation produced a real violation (see Section 11) and was tightened mid-build.
Three further allocations were corrected by hand after spot-checking the full 165-row
allocation (see Section 11). Pages where no acceptable image existed for a slot were left
with fewer images rather than forced into a bad match, per the governing instructions'
explicit permission to use fewer images when relevant ones are unavailable.

================================================================================
4. PER-PAGE IMAGE COUNTS
================================================================================
32 pages received images (165 total). Target counts were ~5–10 for service/area pages and
~3–5 for special pages (Contact/FAQ/Cost/About/Services/Service-Areas), adjusted down in a
few cases to the page's real available-image depth. See image-page-summary.csv for the
full per-page table. Three pages ended up below their initial flexible target, each for a
disclosed, deliberate reason rather than an oversight:
  - cost.html: 2 images (target 3). The page's second existing placeholder slot described
    "Plumber reviewing a repair estimate with a homeowner on a tablet" — no photo in the
    556-image library matches that specific scene well enough to pass the relevance gate,
    so the slot was left unfilled and its placeholder markup removed rather than shipped
    as a visible gray box.
  - service-areas.html: 2 images (target 3). One existing slot explicitly called for a
    "map-style graphic showing Sandy, Utah and its surrounding service area." No real
    photograph can honestly substitute for a map graphic, so this slot was deliberately
    left empty rather than filled with an unrelated photo. A real map/service-area graphic
    is a design-asset need, not a photo-selection problem, and is flagged as follow-up
    work in Section 17.
  - index.html (homepage): 3 images (target 5). The homepage's <main> content is almost
    entirely built from custom componentized sections (service-row list, FAQ accordion,
    elevation-pressure interactive widget, note cards, ZIP-code chips, brand strip) with
    essentially no generic paragraph flow to insert into safely. The automated insertion-
    point finder correctly returned 0 safe candidates here. Three images were hand-placed
    at the only genuinely safe section boundaries (between the services list and the
    pressure section; between the pressure section and the ground/soil section; between
    the coverage-area section and the FAQ section), respecting the hero rule (nothing
    before the H1/lede/first CTA) and never interrupting a componentized block.

11. RELEVANCE FINDINGS AND CORRECTIONS MADE DURING BUILD
================================================================================
Three real relevance problems were found during manual spot-checking of the full
allocation (before HTML was written) and corrected by hand:
  a) gas-line-repair.html's third slot had been matched to an unrelated sewer-wye fitting
     photo purely on a weak generic-keyword overlap, because the gas-specific photo pool
     (only 2 true gas-line images exist in the whole 556-image library) was exhausted.
     This triggered the acceptance-gate tightening described in Section 3.
  b) After tightening, a second-pass run still matched gas-line-repair.html's "pressure
     testing a residential gas line" slot to an unrelated pool-plumbing pressure-test
     photo (that file carries no topic tag at all and only coincidentally shared two
     generic words with the surrounding text). It was swapped by hand for the site's other
     genuine gas-valve photo, reclaimed from faqs.html.
  c) faqs.html's "checking a water shutoff valve" FAQ slot had, in turn, picked up a photo
     that is specifically a GAS shutoff valve beside a water heater — accurate to its own
     gas_line/water_heater_repair tags, but not a true "water shutoff valve" photo. It was
     swapped for a genuine water-heater shutoff-valve photo instead (freed up by fix b).
  d) service-areas.html's "map-style graphic" slot (see Section 4) was deliberately left
     unfilled rather than matched to any real photo.
All four corrections are reflected in the final allocation and in image-placement-
manifest.csv.

Area-page limitation (disclosed, not a defect): the 556-image library contains no
location-specific photography (no street signs, landmarks, or neighborhood-identifiable
exteriors) for Cottonwood Heights, Sandy Hills, Pepperwood, Hidden Valley, White City, or
Willow Creek. Six existing IMAGE_SLOT_META placeholders on those pages originally called
for hyper-local shots (e.g. "Service van parked near Dimple Dell Regional Park
trailhead," "Welcome sign for the city of White City, Utah"). These slots were filled
instead with genuinely relevant, generic plumbing-work photography matching each page's
actual topic (e.g. frozen-pipe repair work for Sandy Hills' freeze-risk content, water-
softener installation for White City's hard-water content) rather than left empty or
filled with a fabricated "looks local" substitute. No image on these pages claims a
specific capture location it does not have.

================================================================================
PASSED, verified technically: Sandy, UT service-area coordinates (40.5724, -111.8599,

these as general content photography representing the Sandy, UT service area, not as
geotagged photos of specific job sites.
