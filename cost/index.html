How We Price a Job

Every range on this page reflects what a comparable job has actually run in this market recently, not a theoretical minimum meant to win a phone call. A low number quoted just to get in the door, then revised upward once a technician is standing in the garage, is not how pricing works here. Each range runs from the low end of a straightforward job to the high end of a more complicated one, with nothing hidden in between.

A price gets confirmed against the actual job once a technician has seen it, not before. Two jobs with the same name, a water heater replacement at $1,800 versus one at $2,800, a slab leak repair at $500 versus one needing a full reroute near $4,000, can land at opposite ends of the same range depending on what gets found once the work starts. That difference gets walked through before any work begins, not discovered on the invoice.

Financing and payment timing questions come up often enough to state plainly here: this page shows the range itself, not a payment plan or a promotional rate. Those details get discussed directly with dispatch, not advertised on a page that cannot account for one household's specific situation.

What Can Move a Quote Up or Down

Access is the single biggest swing factor on almost every job here. A water heater in an easily reached garage closet costs less to replace than the same unit wedged into a tight attic crawlspace. A slab leak reachable through an open closet floor costs less to repair than one requiring a core cut through finished tile in an occupied room.

The home's age and layout matter nearly as much. A whole-house repipe on a single-story slab home with simple drywall access runs toward the lower end of its range. The same job on a two-story home with finished ceilings below the plumbing runs toward the higher end, purely because of how much drywall, insulation, and finish work has to come down and go back up around the pipe itself.

Permits and inspections add a real, specific cost on jobs that require them, water heater installation chief among them under Grapevine's current code, typically $75 to $150 for the permit itself. That cost is included in the estimate from the start, never tacked on later once the permit office gets involved.

Pipe material and line length matter most on sewer and gas line work specifically. A trenchless pipe lining job scales directly with linear footage and pipe diameter. A gas line extension scales with how far the new run has to travel and how much pressure testing it requires before it gets signed off.

Not Sure Which Range Applies?

Have a job that does not fit neatly into one of these categories, or a combination of more than one? Call and describe what is going on. Dispatch can usually give a realistic range over the phone once the actual symptoms and the home's rough layout are known.
