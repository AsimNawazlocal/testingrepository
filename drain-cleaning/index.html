Why Sandy's older lines clog differently

Hard water (9.1 grains per gallon) narrows the usable width of older galvanized and cast-iron lines over time, so a partly-closed pipe clogs on far less debris. Mature trees near Sandy's historic core have also had decades to find their way into aging clay joints — a repeat main-line clog here is more often root intrusion or scale than a one-time object.

How often to schedule it

  • Most homes: preventative cleaning every 18–24 months.
  • Mature trees near the line, or heavy kitchen grease use: annual hydro jetting instead — see our Hydro Jetting page.

Commercial kitchens: what's different

  • Grease traps and kitchen exhaust drains need scheduled cleaning, not just reactive clog clearing, to stay compliant and avoid a mid-service backup.

What a camera inspection actually shows us

  • Whether the clog is a one-time object or ongoing mineral scale narrowing the pipe.
  • Root intrusion through a joint or crack, which changes the conversation toward a line repair.
  • Whether the issue sits in a single branch or the shared main line.

How a visit works

  • 1. Describe the symptom: which drains are affected, and whether it's new or repeat.
  • 2. Clear the line: cabling for most calls, camera-guided where needed.
  • 3. Camera confirmation: we show you the cleared line, not just tell you it's clear.
  • 4. Recommend next steps: routine maintenance schedule, or a referral to jetting or line repair if the camera shows a bigger issue.

Simple habits that cut down on clogs

  • Keep grease, coffee grounds, and food scraps out of the kitchen drain, even with a disposal installed.
  • Use a hair catcher in tub and shower drains.
  • Have outdoor storm drains checked before the fall if mature trees drop leaves near them.

Storm & French drains around the property

Exterior drainage lines carrying water away from a foundation or yard low point clog with leaves, silt, and sediment rather than the grease and hair that clog interior lines. A storm drain that stops draining after a snowmelt or heavy rain is usually a surface-level blockage at the grate or a short run of silted pipe, and it's typically a faster fix than an interior main-line clog.

If a clog keeps coming back

A drain that clears and returns within a few weeks, more than once, usually means the cabling cleared the symptom without addressing the cause. That pattern is when we'll recommend a camera inspection before clearing it a third time, so the next visit fixes the actual problem instead of repeating the same temporary result.

Bathroom drains and slow-draining tubs

A tub or shower drain that empties slower than it used to is almost always hair and soap scum built up just past the trap, which a cable clears quickly. If it's paired with a sulfur smell or gurgling from a nearby toilet, that combination points further down the line, and we'll check the branch connection rather than just clear the visible clog.

Garbage disposal and drain clogs

A kitchen drain that clogs repeatedly right at the disposal connection is sometimes a disposal problem wearing the disguise of a drain problem. If cabling clears it but it's back within days, ask us to check the disposal's discharge connection while we're there — see our garbage disposal page if that turns out to be the actual cause.

When a clog isn't really a clog

Gurgling drains, slow draining across multiple fixtures at once, or a toilet that bubbles when the washing machine drains can point to a venting problem rather than a blockage. Plumbing vents let air into the drain system so water can flow freely; a blocked or undersized vent produces symptoms that look like a clog but won't clear with cabling alone. We'll flag this possibility on a repeat-symptom call rather than keep cabling a line that isn't actually obstructed.

DIY drain cleaners: why we don't recommend them

Chemical drain cleaners can damage older pipe materials, particularly aging galvanized or cast-iron lines common in Sandy's older housing stock, and they make a subsequent professional cleaning more hazardous since the residue can splash back during cabling. If a drain hasn't cleared after one reasonable attempt with a plunger, calling for a cable-and-camera visit is both safer for the pipe and usually faster than escalating through a series of chemical treatments that may not even address the actual cause.

Floor drains in garages and utility rooms

Floor drains see infrequent use compared to a kitchen or bathroom drain, which means their trap can dry out and let sewer gas odor into the room even without an actual clog. Pouring a cup of water down an unused floor drain every month or two keeps the trap sealed and is a simple habit that prevents a surprising number of "mystery smell" calls.
