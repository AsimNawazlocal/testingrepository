Gas Line Repair & Leak Detection in Grapevine, TX

A gas smell is always urgent. We find the leak, isolate it, and repair it with a technician who carries the specific gas endorsement Texas requires on top of a standard plumbing license.

What This Service Includes

A rotten-egg smell near an appliance or along a buried line is not something to schedule around. The response is always the same: get everyone out, do not flip a switch or light anything on the way, and call both the gas utility's emergency line and a licensed plumber from outside the house. On site, we find the exact point of the leak with a combustible-gas detector and a soap test before anything gets opened up.

Gas work in Texas needs its own endorsement beyond a standard plumbing license, because the safety margin is smaller than it is on a water line. Every repair and new connection in Grapevine follows the city's adopted 2021 International Fuel Gas Code, effective January 1, 2024, which sets the sizing, venting, and testing requirements we work to. This page covers two things: repairing or replacing gas piping from the meter to an appliance connection, and leak detection specific to gas, which uses different tools than the acoustic equipment we use for water leaks.

Before any digging near a buried line, we call Texas811 to get every underground utility marked. That is state law, not a courtesy step, and it protects against hitting a line that moved since it was mapped. The locate request is built into the job timeline, which is one reason a gas line repair rarely happens in the same hour as the first call, the way a simple fixture leak can.

New or relocated appliance connections, a range, a dryer, an outdoor grill, a pool heater, fall on this page too, even when no leak is involved. Each one gets sized to that appliance's actual BTU demand rather than copied from whatever line already fed a similar fixture somewhere else in the house.

A carbon monoxide concern gets the same urgency as a gas smell, even though they are different hazards. A malfunctioning appliance can produce one without the other being obvious. We check appliance venting and combustion on the same visit, not just the supply line, because a line that tests leak-free does not rule out a problem at the appliance itself.

Grapevine-Specific Factors

A large share of Grapevine homes built from the 1990s on, especially the subdivisions toward Flower Mound and along Highway 114, run natural gas for the water heater, the range, and often an outdoor kitchen or fire feature. More gas connection points per house means more places a fitting or line can eventually need attention.

Older gas lines sometimes used materials or fittings no longer considered acceptable practice. A repair on an older Grapevine property can turn into a conversation about bringing a section of line up to current code, not just fixing one fitting, especially on a house that has had no gas work done since it was built. A home sale often triggers this exact inspection: a buyer's lender or inspector flags aging gas piping before closing, which is why a pre-listing check is common practice among sellers in the city's older neighborhoods.

What Happens On-Site

On a reported leak, the first priority is finding and isolating the source, sometimes by shutting gas off to the whole house at the meter until the exact failure point is found. On planned work, a Texas811 locate is requested before any digging, and the scope, materials, and code requirements get explained before work starts, not discovered once the trench is already open. After a repair, the line is pressure tested, and we coordinate directly with the gas utility on any reconnection or meter work rather than leaving that step to the homeowner.

Quick Checklist While You Wait

  • Leave the house immediately if you smell gas. Do not flip a light switch, unplug anything, or light a match on the way out.
  • Call the gas utility's emergency line and a licensed plumber from outside the home, not from inside it.
  • Do not try to find or seal the leak yourself. Pinpointing a gas leak safely takes a combustible-gas detector, not a soap bottle and a guess.
  • If you hear a hissing sound near the meter or smell gas outdoors near a buried line, keep vehicles and open flames away from the area until a technician arrives.
  • Carbon monoxide has no smell. A working CO detector near sleeping areas is the only reliable warning if a gas appliance is venting improperly.
