The Scope of This Job in Grapevine

North Texas sits on expansive clay. It swells after rain and shrinks in a dry summer, every single year. That movement works on a concrete slab the same way it works on a foundation, and the copper or CPVC line cast into that slab takes the strain with it. A slab leak here is rarely a one-off. It is closer to a predictable outcome of the soil, especially in homes built on post-tension slabs through the 1990s and 2000s, where tensioned cables add one more reason not to dig before knowing exactly where the leak sits.

Detection comes before any decision about repair. Every fixture gets shut off, then the water meter gets watched. If the dial keeps turning with nothing running, water is leaving the system under the house. From there, an acoustic listening disc works along the accessible run, hot water isolated at the heater first, because most of the slab leaks we find in Grapevine are on the hot line. Thermal imaging can back that up on an exposed slab section, but it is a secondary check, not the primary method.

Once the leak sits within a tight radius, there are two honest options. A spot repair means one core cut at that point, patched and pressure tested, right when the rest of the line is otherwise sound. A full reroute abandons the slab run and brings new pipe through the attic and down the walls instead. It costs more and involves some drywall work, but it ends the slab-leak conversation for good on a line that has already failed more than once.

A copper slab line with one spot repair already behind it is statistically more likely to leak again elsewhere on the same run within a few years, since whatever caused the first failure, corrosion or soil movement, has not gone anywhere. That history is part of the honest conversation at estimate time. A second or third repair on the same line is still an option, but a reroute starts to make more financial sense once a home is on its second slab leak, not its first.

What's Different About This in Grapevine

Insurance carriers in this part of Texas increasingly ask for a plumber's written leak report, including the detection method used and the point of failure, before a slab-leak claim moves forward. That report is prepared as part of the job, not as a separate add-on, since it is often the difference between a claim that proceeds smoothly and one that stalls on missing documentation.

Grapevine's older subdivisions built before post-tension slabs became standard tend to have a simpler repair path, since a core cut does not risk cutting a tensioned cable. Homes from the late 1980s onward, especially the denser infill near Historic Main Street and the newer phases out past William D. Tate Avenue, are far more likely to be post-tension, which is exactly why detection has to pin the leak down precisely before any cutting starts rather than opening a wider exploratory section.

How This Job Usually Goes

Expect the visit to start with a conversation, not a tool: which fixtures have been used, whether the sound of running water has been noticed with everything off, and whether the water bill has moved. From there, isolation testing narrows the search to hot or cold, acoustic equipment narrows it further to a location, and the proposed fix, spot repair or reroute, gets explained in plain terms with a price before anything is cut. Work on the slab itself only begins once that plan is agreed to.

What This Page Doesn't Cover

A warm patch on the floor or an unexplained water bill without any visible water is the territory this page covers. A leak that is visible, dripping, or clearly above the slab, a supply line under a sink or behind a washing machine, belongs on Water Leak Detection instead, which handles the broader, non-foundation leak search.

Something We're Asked Often

What are the most common signs of a hidden slab leak in Grapevine homes?

Key indicators include unexpected spikes in your monthly water bill, warm spots on tile or flooring, the sound of running water when all taps are off, and hairline cracks in baseboards or foundation slabs. Professional acoustic leak detection is required to pinpoint the exact pipe location.
